Stagepack: how to give a presentation

You have two files: Stagepack-Setup.exe and the presentation, a file ending in .sptx.

1. Download both files to your computer first.
   Do not open the presentation straight from the email or from the Teams preview: save both files to a
   folder (for example Downloads), then work from there.

2. Run Stagepack-Setup.exe, once.
   Double-click it and answer Yes. It needs no administrator rights.
   If Windows says "Windows protected your PC", click "More info", then "Run anyway".
   The installer is not signed with a purchased certificate, so Windows does not know it yet. That message
   is expected.

3. Double-click the .sptx file.
   The first start can take a moment; "Opening …" is shown meanwhile.
   The presenter view then opens in a window of its own (Chrome or Edge, without tabs), with the speaker
   notes.
   To show the slides to the audience, click the screen icon in the presenter view, or use the Stagepack
   icon in the tray (bottom right, next to the clock; it may be under the small arrow) and choose
   "Open audience view". Drag that window to the projector.

4. Your notes are saved.
   Edit the speaker notes as you like. They are written into the .sptx file itself. Hover over or click
   the Stagepack tray icon to see "Notes saved".

5. To finish, use the tray icon and choose "Stop", or close the presentation's windows.
   Stagepack saves the notes one last time and closes the presentation.

Save a copy…
   In the tray menu, "Save a copy…" saves the presentation with your notes under another name; notes you
   edit after that go into the copy.
   If the tray says "Notes not saved in the file", the .sptx file could not be written (it is read-only,
   or the USB stick was removed). Your notes are kept; use "Save a copy…" to put them in a new file.
   If it says "Temporary copy", the presentation was opened from an email or a preview: use "Save a copy…"
   to keep your notes.

The presentation runs on your own computer. It needs no internet connection, unless its author left its
fonts to be loaded from the web; then the slides still work, with other fonts.

"… contains code"
   Some presentations come with code of their own (for example an interactive part of a slide). That code
   runs on your computer, with access to your files, while the presentation is shown. Before it starts such
   a presentation, Stagepack shows what the code is and asks whether to run it.
   Choose Yes only if you trust the person who sent you the file. Stagepack then remembers your answer
   for this version of the presentation, so reopening it does not ask again (editing notes does not count
   as a change). If the presentation is changed later, you are asked again.
   Choose No if you are unsure: the presentation is closed and nothing is run. Ask its author about it.
   Presentations without code of their own open without this question.

To remove Stagepack: Settings → Apps → Stagepack → Uninstall. Your .sptx files are not touched.

On Linux (Ubuntu and other desktops)
   There is no setup program. Run "stagepack install" from the Linux build folder once, in a terminal; it
   needs no sudo. Then double-click the .sptx file. The Stagepack icon is at the top right of the screen,
   with the same menu as above (Open presenter, Open audience view, Save a copy…, Stop).
   The "… contains code" question needs zenity (installed on Ubuntu). Without it the presentation is not
   opened.
   To remove Stagepack: run ~/.local/share/stagepack/stagepack uninstall. Your .sptx files are not touched.
